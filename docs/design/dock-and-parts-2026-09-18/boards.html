Board 1 · The dock, reassessed what is there today, what Fluent's CommandBar says, and the proposal at full width

The dock is four floating acrylic groups over the drawing, icon-only, with a NavigationView pill under the tool in hand and no overflow. Fluent's CommandBar is the pattern it is closest to: one surface, commands with an icon and a label, labels collapse before commands do, and commands that no longer fit go into a "…" overflow — never away. The proposal keeps what earns its place (floating, acrylic, the accent Take off, the scope as a control) and fixes seven things the code does that the pattern does not.

Findings, against Fluent's CommandBar

  1. Four surfaces for one bar. Four shadows, four strokes and three gaps sit on the sheet; the eye counts islands before it reads controls. A CommandBar is one surface whose groups are separators. Proposed: two surfaces — what you are doing (read tools · scope · takeoff tools) and where you are (sheet · zoom · scale). The scale group, a whole surface for one control, joins the sheet it describes. Dock.tsx: four .dockgroup
  2. No labels, ever. Fluent shows icon + label when there is room and drops the label first, the command last. Five takeoff tools an estimator meets weekly are icons with tooltips at every width. Proposed: labels at Spacious on the tools, Direction and Done; icons from Standard down; tooltips always.
  3. The tool in hand wears a navigation pill. The 16×3 accent bar is NavigationView's selected page mark, borrowed for a toggle. An AppBarToggleButton that is checked is a fill (ControlFillColorSecondary) and nothing under it. Proposed: the fill, with the Filled glyph in the accent — the rule the rail already follows. .dockbtn[aria-pressed]::after
  4. Collapse discards commands. Under 900px the zoom steppers vanish; under 560px Fit; under 400px every takeoff tool — and there is no overflow menu ("There is no overflow menu any more", shell.css). Fluent moves what does not fit into "…"; it never hides a command. Proposed: each surface owns a "…" that fills as tiers collapse (board 2).
  5. The scope name yields first. With 1400px of drawing the pill shows "C-MT-01 Met…" because it is the only flexible thing in a row of fixed ones. The scope is where markups land — the one context worth reading. Proposed: a 160px floor and a 240px ceiling; it truncates last, and the read tools go behind "…" before it does.
  6. Leaving takeoff is ×. Dismiss, at the end of a row of drawing tools, next to Direction, reads as "undo the last one". Proposed: Take off and Done are one slot with two states — the accent button becomes an outlined Done in the same place.
  7. Zoom is four controls. −, value, +, Fit. The value already opens a menu with both fits and presets. Proposed: at Standard the steppers stay (they are used); at Compact they go to the menu and Ctrl±; Fit stays to Minimum because it is the one pressed most.

Kept on purpose: floating over the drawing (Whiteboard, Figma), acrylic, 32px buttons on a 40px surface (WinUI compact CommandBar), the accent Take off as the one primary, Pan · Select · Dimension as read tools apart from the scope's tools.

Board 4 · The parts list, audited what each product type emits today (from the code), and the minimal list proposed

Two lists share the scope page: the number tiles (what was measured: SF, LF, EA) and Parts (what is ordered). Today the tiles always show three slots, Area · Perimeter · Count, with "—" in whatever this scope does not measure, and Parts mixes measurements into the order list. The rule proposed: a tile is a measurement, a part is a thing you buy, and nothing appears twice. Rows tinted red are duplicates; rows tinted yellow are measurements sitting in the order list.

Rules, in one place

A tile is a measurement. Area, Perimeter, Length, Installed, Count — what the markups add up to, in SF, LF or EA. A scope shows only the tiles its type measures; no slot is ever empty.

A part is a thing you buy. Panels, planks, baffle stock, cassettes, linear parts. One line each, with its size as a chip and its arithmetic (breakdown, ordered length, offcut) as a quiet second line — never as a part of its own.

Accessories are a second group. Rails, connectors, end caps, joiners, trim: real, but not the product. Folded under their own header, only the ones above zero.

Nothing twice. Full panels at yield "full", measured length beside the Length tile, installed length in the parts list — each is the same number in a second place, and each goes.

Confidence is said once. An unverified product gets one chip on the Parts header, not a note on every line. The export keeps confidence as a column, as it does now.

No parts, no section. A custom assembly is quantified by hand: the tile is the whole answer and "No parts for this product" is a sentence about the software, not the ceiling.

The data does not change. takeoff.ts and runs.ts keep emitting every quantity; bom.ts keeps every line and its confidence; the TSV and HTML exports gain a "kind" (part · detail · accessory · measurement) rather than losing rows. This is a presentation rule, so a number that exists today still exists.
