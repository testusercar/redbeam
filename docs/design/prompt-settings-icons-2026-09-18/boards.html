1 · The command prompt rethought from first principles · a Raycast, not a terminal

A prompt has one job: get the estimator to the thing they mean in as few keystrokes as possible, then let them act on it. Raycast gets that right with four moves that the current prompt lacks: nouns and verbs are separate (a scope is one row, its actions are one keystroke deeper, not five rows of "Take off in…", "Rename…", "Colour…"), the empty state is suggestions, not a catalogue (what fits the sheet you are on, then what you ran last), filtering is a control, not a cheat-sheet (the "All ▾" menu replaces the seven prefixes as the visible affordance; the prefixes still work for anyone who learned them), and nothing shouts: an item that cannot run right now is hidden, or when you searched for it by name, greyed with its reason in the subtitle, never amber.

2 · Settings sixteen switches do not need a navigation pane

What is wrong now: a category rail, a breadcrumb, a search box, a reset button and a folder icon on every card, wrapped around sixteen settings, four of which live alone on their own page. The architecture is Windows Settings; the content is one screen. The rethink: one page, eight titled runs, a 44px row per setting, the description one line, the control on the right, the five layout-preview switches drawn as the family they are. A changed setting shows a 2px accent bar and an inline "Default" link; "Reset all" survives in the header. The filter field at the top and the prompt's = mode find a setting by any word in it. About is the last row, not a page.

Rows, not cards. A card per setting made 16 settings look like 16 features. A row per setting reads as a list you scan.

No icons on rows. The folder on every General card carried no information. Icons return only where they distinguish: none of these do.

Descriptions in one line. Two-line explanations become a tooltip on hover and the prompt's detail text. The row keeps its 44px.

The family is drawn. Five layout-preview switches hang off their parent with a connector and dim together when it is off.

Changed is visible. A 2px accent bar and an inline "Default 40" link, the VS Code convention, instead of a count in the corner.

Same primitives as the sidebar. ToggleSwitch, compact ComboBox, NumberBox with a spinner, 28px section heads.

3 · Iconography one glyph per noun, one per verb, filled when active

The family is right (Fluent, drawn per size) and the sizes are right. What is wrong is the vocabulary: a chevron stands for every command in the prompt, a pentagon stands for an area, a cutout and a shape alike, a calculator stands for an estimate, a folder decorates every settings card, and the rail's four buttons read as generic. The fix is a small dictionary, held in one file, that the whole app draws from: every noun has a glyph, every verb has a glyph, a thing that is selected or active switches from the Regular weight to the Filled one (the Windows 11 rule), and a glyph never appears where it distinguishes nothing.

4 · The no-scale warning only where it is true of something

Today the bar appears on any open sheet without a scale, including the title sheet and the schedules, where there is nothing drawn for a scale to apply to. A warning about nothing trains people to ignore the bar. The rule: the bar appears only on a sheet that has markups and no scale, it names the sheet and the count, and it carries the one action that fixes it. Elsewhere the sheet's state is where it belongs already: the dock's scale control reads "Unset".
